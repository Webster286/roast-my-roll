2. What the app processes on your device

To create your roast, the app asks for access to your photo library. You grant this permission through the iOS system dialogue and can change or withdraw it at any time under Settings → Apps → roast my roll → Photos.

With your permission, the app reads the following – on your device only:

  • date and time taken, media type (photo, video, screenshot, Live Photo, portrait) and video length,
  • which albums and iOS collections (e.g. Selfies, Favourites) items belong to, when you choose an album or start your year in review,
  • the location data stored in a sample of your photos – to count places, work out distances and show the nearest place from a place table built into the app (e.g. “Munich” or “near Augsburg”). This happens entirely on your device; no coordinates are transmitted and your exact address is not determined,
  • individual photos (e.g. the first and last picture, or one from your “record day”) to show them on the cards. You can switch this off before the analysis and swap or remove photos on each card,
  • for a sample of your photos, the image content using iOS image recognition (Apple Vision): general subjects (e.g. food, pets, sky, nature), an estimate of image quality, how similar consecutive photos are and the average colour. This produces cards such as “Your best photo” or “Foodie rate”. People are neither recognised nor identified.

For the “photo dump”, you pick the pictures yourself using the iOS photo picker. The app only receives the pictures you select and uses them solely to create the collage on your device.

All results exist only in the app’s working memory while you use it. They are neither stored permanently nor transmitted to me or anyone else. There is no facial recognition and no identification of people.

As this processing happens entirely on your device and without any access by me, I do not collect any personal data in the process.

3. Sharing

Content only leaves the app when you actively tap “Share”. An image of the card is then created and handed to the iOS share sheet. If a card contains photos, the app first asks whether you want to share them, blur them or cancel. Where you share (e.g. Instagram, TikTok, WhatsApp) is up to you; that service’s privacy policy applies there.

The built-in place table is based on data from GeoNames.org, licensed under CC BY 4.0.

The optional duel code only contains rounded figures (e.g. number of photos, screenshot share, photo type) – no photos, no places, no names.

4. Clipboard

The app only reads the clipboard when you explicitly tap “Paste from clipboard” in the duel section. It only writes to it when you choose to copy your code or share a card directly to your Instagram story – the card image then stays on the clipboard for at most five minutes so that Instagram can pick it up.

5. Monthly reminder

If you choose “Remind me on the 1st of every month”, iOS schedules a local notification on your device. For this, the app asks once for permission to send notifications. Nothing is transmitted to me or anyone else; you can turn the reminder off at any time in the app or in iOS Settings.

6. Apple App Store

The app is distributed through Apple’s App Store. Apple processes data for downloads and any purchases under its own responsibility; Apple’s privacy policy applies. If you have agreed in iOS Settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple provides me with aggregated, non-personal statistics and crash reports. I have no influence over this and cannot identify individuals from it.

If you write to me, I process your email address and the content of your message in order to answer your enquiry (Art. 6(1)(b) or (f) GDPR). The data is deleted once it is no longer needed for this purpose and no statutory retention obligations apply.
